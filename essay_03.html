On noticing too hard

I have a problem. I notice things. Not like, a superpower — more like a mild affliction that makes movies harder to watch and group chats exhausting.

It goes like this: someone says something, and I notice the way they said it. The pause before the word. The thing they almost said and then didn't. The micro-expression that happened when a specific topic came up. And then I spend the next twenty minutes thinking about it while the conversation has moved on completely and everyone else is talking about something else and I'm still back there going wait, but what did that mean.

It's fine. It's mostly fine.

My friends have learned to ask "are you noticing something right now?" when I go quiet. The answer is almost always yes.

The thing about noticing too hard is that you can't really turn it off. You can redirect it, maybe. Channel it into something. I started writing things down — just small observations, in notes apps, on napkins, in the margins of whatever I was reading. Not because I thought they'd become anything. Just because they needed somewhere to go.

A lot of them are embarrassingly mundane. The way this person always apologizes before asking for something, even small things. Why does this café arrange the furniture so that you can't sit without your back to the door. That movie ended and I feel like something important happened but I can't name it yet.

Some of them turned into essays. A few turned into research questions. One turned into a whole paper about how investigation structures shape what people are allowed to conclude. That one surprised me.

Ratatouille is the best movie ever made, by the way. "Anyone can cook" is not about cooking. I think about it at least once a week.

I think the noticing used to feel like a problem — like I was somehow doing attention wrong, spending it on the wrong things. Now I think it's just how I'm built. Some people hear a song and remember where they were. I notice a thing and can't stop thinking about what it means.

Mostly I've made peace with it. It makes me a decent researcher. A slightly intense person to have a conversation with. Someone who will remember the exact thing you said six months ago and bring it up at a strange moment.

But honestly? The noticing is the best part. The thing before the conclusion, before you've named it or filed it or explained it away. Just the raw fact of: huh. that's interesting.

I'm trying to stay there longer. 🌀
