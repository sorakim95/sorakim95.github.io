The barista years

I made lattes for three years. I also learned more about people in those three years than in any class I've ever taken.

The thing about working at a café is that you become completely invisible. Not in a sad way — in a useful way. People forget you're there. They have full conversations in front of you, make faces at their phones, argue with their partners in low voices that aren't quite low enough. You're just the person making the coffee.

I noticed everything.

There was a regular who came in every Tuesday at 10:47am — never 10:45, never 11:00 — and always ordered a flat white, and always looked slightly disappointed when she got it, even though she ordered the same thing every time. I spent maybe six Tuesdays thinking about what she was actually hoping for. I never figured it out. I still think about her sometimes.

The latte art took about four months to get decent. Rosetta first, then tulips. I'm still working on swans. Swans are cruel.

What I actually learned, though — the useful thing — was how to read a situation before anyone said anything. Who needed the coffee fast and who needed someone to make eye contact for two seconds. Who was having a terrible day and just needed the transaction to go smoothly. Who wanted to chat and who would die if you tried.

You get very good at noticing what people actually need versus what they say they want. Sometimes those are the same thing. Often they're not.

I think about this a lot in my research now — the gap between what a system is designed to do and what people actually do inside it. A procedure says: follow these steps. A person says: okay. And then they adapt, improvise, ask a colleague instead of checking the SOP, skip the form because it's faster, find workarounds that work until they don't.

This is not a metaphor I planned. It just happened. Three years of watching people order coffee will do that to you.

Anyway. I left the café eventually. Traded the apron for a GMP uniform and a lot of paperwork. Still making lattes though, just for myself now. Still noticing things.

The Tuesday regular would have liked that, I think. ☕
